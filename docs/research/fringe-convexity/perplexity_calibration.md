## A. Fill model (convex_fill_sim) — the napkin shape is not defensible; calibrate to our own fills

- **Replace the linear "willingness × availability" with a hazard/survival model.** Standard form: execution intensity falls with distance-to-touch, Avellaneda–Stoikov `λ(d)=A·e^{-k·d}` (or power-law), finite-horizon `P(fill by T)=1−e^{−λT}`. The resulting fill-vs-offset curve is **sigmoidal** (low → steep → saturating), NOT linear. Our `w(f)=passive+(1−passive)·f` is not a defensible general shape.
- **`passive=0.15` is a fixed floor with no horizon / queue meaning.** And compounding a per-step prob across 4 re-pegs as independent trials inflates the cumulative — worse, **a re-peg does not retain queue priority**. Use sequential survival accrual and model priority reset on reprice.
- **Our availability double-counts spread.** Spread has two roles: a price-cost scale (concession `f·half`) AND the distance/market-state. Penalizing `spread_bps` separately from `d=(1−f)·half` double-counts. Displayed depth at the price + contra-flow matter more than OI; OI/volume are weak proxies, not a fill-prob conversion.
- **Our reference magnitudes are arbitrary, not literature-calibrated.** `SPREAD_PENALTY_BPS=12000` ≈ 120% of mid (barely bites many wide quotes; 0.1 floor guarantees nonzero); `LIQ_REF=1500` / the `5×vol` weight / the 0.2 floor all need fitting to instrument + horizon + order size.
- **Benchmarks (reference, not transferable to thin wings):** SEC SPXW 0DTE — midpoint customer orders ~40–71% complete-fill at 1s; price-improvement aggressiveness gives steep nonlinear curves (e.g. 13/52/87% across improvement levels). Options exec study — quoted half-spread ~9.7c, effective ~6.5c, price improvement ~2.9c (~50% of the half-spread). Retail equity limit orders — inside-quote 83% fill / ~30s, at-quote 76% / ~95s. These are active-options / stock / full-sample numbers, NOT a thin far-OTM wing fill rate.
- **Our range check:** 0.14 (thin) plausible-but-maybe-still-optimistic (stale quote, small size, priority loss on re-peg); 0.9 (liquid) only for aggressive/active, needs our own data. Cost at cap `f=0.5` = half the half-spread = a quarter of the full spread, before quote drift/adverse selection. **Report cents/contract AND fraction-of-spread AND bps-of-premium separately.**
- **Robinhood is not a public FIFO book** — routes on historical execution performance; limits not guaranteed (partial/no fill). Calibrate to RH order-level outcomes, segmented by spread-in-ticks, premium, wing class, time-of-day, size, re-peg schedule.
- **Gate:** v1 fill model needs our own RH fill logs to calibrate — we don't have them yet. Until then `convex_fill_sim` stays a labeled placeholder; keep the sigmoidal-hazard shape as the target, not the current linear one.

## B. Skew fit (replace beta_t / skew_slope placeholders)

- **Fit the observable local smile parsimoniously; do NOT pretend one snapshot identifies β_T or future skew dynamics.**
- **One expiry, 8–15 strikes:** fit a **local quadratic in total variance `w(k)=T·σ²(k)`** around ATM (local linear where curvature isn't supported); estimate the **fringe slope only from nearby reliable strikes**, never by extending the ATM fit to the wing. Use a **Huber loss scaled by quote uncertainty**, or better an **interval loss** (no penalty while the fitted price sits inside the bid–ask). Weight by spread/freshness first, OI/volume secondary + capped. Exclude stale / zero-bid / wide quotes (or loose interval bounds + capped weight).
- Don't default to cubic splines (oscillation) — shape-preserving/monotone if interpolating. SVI is overkill for a local slope at 8–15 points (sparse wings leave wing params poorly constrained); constrained SVI only for a whole surface. Guard non-negative variance + decreasing/convex call prices in strike (butterfly no-arb); check on a dense grid. Monotone IV is a soft prior, not a no-arb condition.
- **β_T is a TIME-SERIES quantity — not from a snapshot.** Estimate from our own synchronized history: `Δσ_ATM = β_T·Δln F + ε`, robust regression, non-overlapping 5–15min obs over ~10–20 sessions, EW decay (~5–10 session half-life), control intraday/event; check a daily-close beta if the destination move is overnight/multi-day.
- **Point vs range:** near-ATM level + slope → point (if liquid strikes bracket ATM, stable to filters). **Fringe slope → RANGE** (thin quotes turn noise into a large fixed-strike reprice). β_T → historical estimate + regime scenarios. **Skew-shape-change → not identified by one snapshot → scenario (incl. zero baseline).**
- Fixed-strike rule `dσ = (β_T − s(k))·dlnF + dσ_shape`: sticky-moneyness β_T=0 → −s·dlnF; sticky-strike β_T≈s(0) → ~0 at ATM; empirical "sticky surface" best for short/medium maturities but varies by underlying/maturity. For a large move, re-evaluate the smile along the path — don't extrapolate one fringe derivative linearly.
- **Biggest traps:** (1) reading cross-sectional skew as the spot→ATM-IV response (it's a time-series quantity); (2) trusting a thin-wing slope; (3) linear-extrapolating a fringe derivative on a big move.

## Net
Both napkin pieces are the right *scaffolding* but the wrong *shape/constants*. The grounded v1: fill = sigmoidal hazard/survival calibrated to our own RH fills (with priority-reset on re-peg, depth/flow features, spread not double-counted); skew = local quad-in-total-variance with interval loss, fringe slope + shape-change carried as ranges/scenarios, β_T from synchronized history. Neither can be truly calibrated until we have our own fill logs + synchronized IV history — so both stay labeled placeholders, now with a concrete target.

## C. Convexity decomposition — vega vs gamma (for the XLK-73DTE anatomy)

- **Clean desk split:** (1) revalue spot + time at FROZEN fixed-strike IV → directional/gamma P&L (`Δ·ΔS + ½Γ·ΔS²`) + theta; (2) revalue at the ENDING IV → surface re-mark; optionally split that into `vega·Δσ + vanna·ΔS·Δσ + ½volga·Δσ²` with a tracked residual. Finite reprice is order-dependent — endpoint repricing is the control.
- **Naming correction (matters for our attribution):** an observed fixed-strike IV rise is primarily **vega·Δσ**, NOT "vanna P&L." Vanna is only the cross term `vanna·ΔS·Δσ`. Don't double-count a smile-adjusted-delta term and the vega re-mark. Watch vega units (per vol-point vs decimal) and the theta sign.
- **Scale at 73 DTE vs 14–30 (BS, delta-matched, illustrative):** at ~0.19Δ gamma ≈ 0.024 (73d) vs 0.055 (14d) — 73-DTE gamma ≈ **44%** of 14-DTE; vega ≈ 0.121 (73d) vs 0.053 (14d) — ≈ **2.3×**. Gamma ~ 1/√T, vega ~ √T at fixed delta. So going to 73 DTE = less gamma, more vega per delta. The 0.05Δ fringe has less of BOTH than the 0.19Δ.
- **vega-P&L / gamma-P&L ≈ 2σT·Δσ / (ΔS/S)²** → grows with tenor for the same shocks, BUT the option is NOT automatically vega-dominated: a small/adverse IV move leaves gamma dominant; only a large favourable IV re-mark makes vega dominate.
- **Honest trade-off:** 73 DTE = larger vega budget + wing depth, but less gamma per spot shock and **material dependence on how the fixed-strike surface actually reprices on the rally** — this is a surface-dynamics *assumption*, not a guarantee. Stress it: sticky-strike (no IV lift → gamma/delta only), measured rally-beta, adverse/vol-crush (IV falls on up-move → positive vega subtracts, vanna cross negative), skew flatten/steepen. "Long call convexity with a larger vega budget and a material dependence on the realised fixed-strike surface response." Convexity after premium/theta/surface is NOT guaranteed.
